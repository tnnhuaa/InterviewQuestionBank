## Slide 029 — SDLC Model [2]

_PDF page 5, handout panel 5_

```text
SDLC Model [2]
• A software life cycle model is either a
descriptive or prescriptive characterization
of software evolution.
• Typically, it is easier and more common to
articulate a prescriptive life cycle model for
how software systems should be developed.
• Descriptive life cycle models, on the other
hand, characterize how particular software
systems are actually developed in specific
settings.
```

## Slide 035 — Why Define a SDLC Model? [2]

_PDF page 6, handout panel 5_

```text
Why Define a SDLC Model? [2]
•
As comparative descriptive or prescriptive accounts for how
software systems come to be the way they are.
•
Regulatory compliance.
•
As frameworks for analyzing or estimating patterns of
resource allocation and consumption during the software life
cycle.
•
To organize, plan, staff, budget, schedule and manage
software project work over organizational time, space, and
computing environments.
•
As a basis for conducting empirical studies to determine
what affects software productivity, cost, and overall quality.
•
As a basis for determining what software engineering tools
and methodologies will be most appropriate to support
different life cycle activities.
•
As prescriptive outlines for what products to produce for
approvals and delivery to client.
•
Establish a framework for building, implementing and
enhancing systems that all personnel have to follow.
```

## Slide 036 — Actually, Why SDLC Model?

_PDF page 6, handout panel 6_

```text
Actually, Why SDLC Model?
• Well-defined model: achieving good and consistent
results, productivity and quality.
• ill-defined model: producing inconsistent results,
overburdening team members with unnecessary
documents, meetings and report requests, leading to less
productivity and quality.
• Is there any proof?
– Productivity = Size / Effort.
– Size = LOC, Classes, Methods, Features, Bugs Fixed
– Effort = Time Log, Hours Billed.
```

## Slide 038 — Another Way to Guarantee the Failure [5]

_PDF page 7, handout panel 2_

```text
Another Way to Guarantee the Failure [5]
• Well-defined roles might help those people to organize
around the work, but the formation of roles is not the goal.
• A checklist might help those people do the work in a way that
meets the goal, but the checklist is not the goal either.
• Confusing processes with the goals is one of the great sins of
management.
```
